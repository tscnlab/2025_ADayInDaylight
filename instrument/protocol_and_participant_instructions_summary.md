# Protocol and participant-instruction summary

## Status and provenance

This deidentified summary is derived from two internal Word documents reviewed on 24 July 2026: a dated planning protocol and a compilation of information communicated to participants. The internal source folder is excluded from version control because it contains contributor names and contact details. No names, personal contact details, individual locations, personalised links or participant responses are reproduced here.

The planning protocol was headed as an “OSF Pre-registration”, and the participant timeline listed preregistration as a task. No OSF registration occurred. The documents are therefore planning and instruction records, not a preregistered protocol.

## Event design

- Technical University of Munich was responsible for the event.
- The event was designed as a globally coordinated personal-light-exposure capture centred on the local calendar day of 22 September 2025.
- The worldwide collection window ran from 21 September 2025 at the UTC+14 dateline through 23 September 2025 at the UTC−12 dateline.
- Sites were invited to contribute at least one person, with additional contributors allowed.
- Each person was asked to wear one logger throughout the applicable local measurement period.

## Wearable instructions

- The final instruction specified an ActLumus or another calibrated light logger worn on the chest using a pin or lanyard.
- The devices were manufacturer calibrated. No project-specific calibration or traceability was required because the event focused on daylight awareness rather than high-accuracy or cross-device measurement fidelity.
- The final recording instruction was 10-second sampling or the lowest interval available on the device. Earlier communication had proposed 30-second sampling.
- Participants were asked to record whenever the logger was removed.
- For nighttime exposure, the protocol instructed participants to place the device on a nightstand facing upwards.

## Context and questionnaire instructions

- Initial communication proposed recording location/environment categories at 10-minute intervals using ATracker.
- Final communication replaced this with MyCap state-change logging: participants were asked to enter a record whenever activity or setting changed, including logger removal.
- An intake questionnaire preceded distribution of personalised MyCap access.
- The participant instructions placed a post-participation survey immediately after the measurement period.
- Participants travelling across time zones were asked to note when time-zone crossings occurred.

The public release retains structured context required to interpret the light measurements. Personalised access links, free text and contact details are excluded. The author-approved participant metadata retain exact age, sex assigned at birth, cleaned country and coordinates rounded to one decimal place while excluding city and exact coordinates. Raw flight numbers are excluded and replaced by opaque dated-flight identifiers, allowing same-flight linkage without exposing the number. Exact demographics, minute-resolution travel intervals and time-zone identifiers passed exact-archive privacy review with residual linkage risk documented.

## Ethics and data sharing

The participant-information compilation states that the project had no prior ethics approval because its primary purpose was a public-outreach event for Daylight Awareness Week, and that a waiver would be sought if the work were published as a research output. The formal TUM institutional waiver is documented in the Data Note.

The planning protocol contemplated deidentified aggregate sharing, participant-level light data after initial analysis and openly shared analysis scripts. No separate standalone consent was collected. Participation in the invited international researcher event was understood as implicit agreement to open and reproducible sharing, and TUM determined that the transformed public release under CC BY 4.0 is permissible. No formal withdrawal procedure or deadline was specified; contributors could withdraw their data at any time without repercussions. The authors approved the released archive and its documented residual linkage risk.
